I received my Ph.D. degree in **Robotics and Autonomous Systems** from the **Hong Kong University of Science and Technology** ([HKUST](https://hkust.edu.hk/)), where I was jointly supervised by [Prof. Jun Ma](https://personal.hkust-gz.edu.cn/junma/index.html) and [Prof. Michael Yu Wang](https://ece.hkust.edu.hk/mywang). Prior to my doctoral studies, I earned the **M.Sc. degree in Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering** from the **University of California, San Diego** ([UCSD](https://ucsd.edu/)) with a major in robot control and motion planning, and the **B.Eng. degree in Mechatronic Engineering** from [Tongji University](https://en.tongji.edu.cn/p/#/).

During my Ph.D. studies, I collaborated with [Prof. Xindong Tang](https://www.math.hkbu.edu.hk/~xdtang/) on the theory and application of **moment and polynomial optimization**, with an emphasis on polynomial formulations and sums-of-squares methods for collision avoidance and trajectory optimization. I also visited the [Computational Robotics Lab](https://computationalrobotics.seas.harvard.edu/) in the **John A. Paulson School of Engineering and Applied Sciences**, **Harvard University**, where I worked with [Prof. Heng Yang](https://hankyang.seas.harvard.edu/) on **numerical optimization** and the development of **high-performance numerical solvers for contact-rich motion planning**.

I was a **Postdoctoral Fellow at the National University of Singapore** ([NUS](https://www.nus.edu.sg/)), where I worked with [Prof. Fan Shi](https://fanshi14.github.io/me/). My postdoctoral research explored **physics-driven world models** that combine differentiable high-fidelity physics simulators with learnable components, with the goal of building physically consistent, end-to-end differentiable models across a broad range of robot–environment interactions.

My research is driven by a long-term goal of **enabling robots to interact with the physical world in a safe, capable, and intelligent manner** — knowing when to avoid contact and when to exploit it. Over the past years, I have built up expertise in generating motion policies for mobile manipulators in complex scenarios, combining optimization-based planning with learning-based methods, with an emphasis on safe and adaptive behavior grounded in **physical models and principled reasoning** — from collision-free navigation in cluttered environments to contact reasoning during interaction.
